DE-ESCALATION TECHNIQUES

1. STAY CALM
   - Control your breathing (slow, deep breaths)
   - Keep your voice low and steady
   - Maintain relaxed body posture

2. ACTIVE LISTENING
   - Let the person speak without interruption
   - Nod to show you are listening
   - Paraphrase back: "What I hear you saying is..."
   - Acknowledge their feelings: "I understand you're upset"

3. NON-THREATENING BODY LANGUAGE
   - Keep hands visible, palms open
   - Maintain safe distance (at least 2 arm lengths)
   - Avoid direct prolonged eye contact
   - Do not point fingers
   - Stand at an angle, not face-to-face

4. VERBAL TECHNIQUES
   - Use "I" statements: "I want to help resolve this"
   - Offer choices: "You can step back or I can leave"
   - Set clear boundaries: "I need you to stop shouting"
   - Avoid absolutes: never say "calm down"
   - Avoid challenging questions: use "Help me understand"

5. WHEN TO DISENGAGE
   - Person becomes physically aggressive
   - You feel unsafe
   - Crowd is becoming hostile
   - Law enforcement arrives and escalates

6. GROUP SAFETY
   - Stay with your team
   - Have an exit route identified
   - Agree on a disengagement signal
   - Document incidents when safe

7. AFTER INCIDENT
   - Debrief with team
   - Note what worked and what didn't
   - Seek support if traumatized
